A LUT can't add light that isn't there. If your face is dark, a lamp in front of you does more than any look.

Add it to OBS

  1. Save the file somewhere it'll stay, like a LUTs folder next to your overlays. If you move it later, OBS loses it.
  2. In OBS, right-click your webcam source → Filters.
  3. Under Effect Filters, click + → Apply LUT, and name it after the look.
  4. Next to Path, click Browse and pick the .cube file.
  5. Set Amount to match the strength you liked here (60% here = 0.60 in OBS).
  6. If you also use Chroma Key, background removal or Sharpen, keep the LUT after the background filters and before Sharpen. Drag filters to reorder them.

Put the filter on the webcam source, not the whole scene, so your overlays and game keep their own colours.

4

Tips

Turn off auto white balance on your camera. Otherwise the camera keeps shifting colour while the LUT stays the same. In OBS: double-click the webcam → Configure Video, and untick Auto next to White Balance (the box varies by camera). Logitech and Elgato cameras also have their own app for this.

Fix the lighting first, then add the look. One soft light in front of you, slightly above eye level, beats every filter. Turn off overhead lights that give you shadows under the eyes.

Check your skin, not the background. If you look orange or grey, lower the strength or try the Fix looks instead of a style.

Same look on every scene. One copy of your webcam with the filter, then Paste (Reference) it into your other scenes, so the look is set once.

The looks are standard 33-point .cube LUTs, so they also work in DaVinci Resolve, Premiere, CapCut and most video editors.
