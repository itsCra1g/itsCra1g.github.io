Sound good on stream, whatever mic you've got.

Viewers will put up with a soft camera, but not bad audio. This guide gets your mic sounding clear and steady in OBS, sets your game, music and alerts at the right levels, and fixes the common problems. Pick your kind of mic below and the settings change to suit it.

1

The 10-minute version

If you only do this, you'll already sound better than most new streams.

  1. Get the mic closeA hand's width from your mouth (about 10 to 15 cm), slightly off to the side so you don't blast it with air.
  2. Set the level at the sourceUse the mic's own gain (knob, app or interface) so talking loudly peaks in the yellow in OBS, never the red.
  3. Use 48 kHz everywhereOBS Settings → Audio → Sample Rate 48 kHz, and the same for your mic and headphones in Windows sound settings.
  4. Add the core filtersNoise Suppression, then Compressor, then Limiter. Add a Gain filter first only if you can't turn the mic up any other way. Settings for your mic are below.
  5. Keep the game under your voiceYour voice should be clearly louder than game sound, which should be clearly louder than music.
  6. Record yourself and listenDo a 30-second test recording with game audio on, then listen back on headphones. It's the only test that counts.
2

What mic have you got?

Pick the closest match. The tips here and the filter settings in section 3 change to suit it.

Where to put it

  • Close beats loud. A mic 10 cm away needs much less gain than one 50 cm away, so it picks up far less keyboard, fan and room.
  • Point it at your mouth and away from noise. Most streaming mics hear best from the front (or the end, for "end-address" mics) and least from the back. Aim the back at your keyboard or PC.
  • Off to the side a little stops "p" and "b" sounds popping. A foam cover or pop filter helps too.
  • Use a boom arm or stand that isn't on your desk if you can, so typing and bumps don't thud through.
  • Soft things help a hard room. Curtains, a rug, a sofa or even a duvet behind you cut echo far better than foam squares on one wall.

These are starting points, not rules. Every voice and room is different. Change one thing at a time and do a test recording after each change.

Setting a noise gate for your room

A gate mutes the mic when you're not talking. Set it too high and it chops the start of your words; too low and it does nothing. To find your number: sit quietly with your PC and game running, and watch your mic's meter in OBS. Type the number it hovers around below.

If the gate cuts off quiet words, lower both numbers by 3. If you can still hear the keyboard between sentences, raise them by 3.

Mic settings outside the filters

  • Mono: most mics are mono but show up in one ear only. Open Edit → Advanced Audio Properties and tick Mono for your mic.
  • Don't hear yourself twice: in the same window, leave your mic's Audio Monitoring on Monitor Off.
  • Turn off unused devices: in Settings → Audio, set any global mic or desktop devices you don't use to Disabled, so nothing sneaks in twice.
  • Audio bitrate: Settings → Output, audio bitrate 160 is a good default for Twitch and Kick.

Turn sources down in OBS, not your headphones. What you hear isn't what viewers hear. Use the OBS fader for each source, then set your own headphones however you like.

Separate the game from everything else

Desktop Audio grabs everything your PC plays: game, Discord, music, browser and every notification. To control each one on its own, add an Application Audio Capture source for each app (Windows 10 version 2004 or later, and Windows 11), then set Desktop Audio to Disabled in Settings → Audio so nothing plays twice. Now the game, Discord and music each get their own fader.

5

Music, VODs and multistreaming

Copyrighted music can get your VODs muted or your channel a strike. Stick to music you have the rights to stream: royalty-free libraries, stream-safe playlists, or your own music. Soundtrack by Twitch closed in 2023, so remove its source if it's still in OBS.

Keep music out of your Twitch VODs

OBS can send Twitch a second audio track just for the VOD, with your music left out. Live viewers hear the music; the VOD doesn't.

  1. Settings → Stream: choose Twitch as the service.
  2. Settings → Output: tick Twitch VOD Track and pick Track 2. (In Simple mode, tick Enable Advanced Encoder Settings first if you don't see it.)
  3. Edit → Advanced Audio Properties: tick Tracks 1 and 2 for your mic, game, Discord and alerts. Tick only Track 1 for music.
  4. Do a short test stream with music on, then check the VOD has no music.

Streaming to Twitch and Kick at once

If you multistream with a plugin such as Multiple RTMP outputs, each extra output has its own audio track setting. Point it at Track 1 (everything) for live viewers. If that platform keeps VODs, think about whether music should be in them too.

6

Fixes for common problems

What you hearUsual cause and fix
Robotic, crackly or "underwater" voiceA sample rate mismatch. Set OBS and every audio device in Windows sound settings to 48 kHz (48000 Hz), then restart OBS.
Viewers hear an echo or everything twiceThe same sound captured twice: Desktop Audio plus an app capture, a mic set to monitor, or speakers picked up by your mic. Disable the duplicate, set monitoring off, wear headphones.
Viewers hear your game or Discord through your micSound leaking from headphones or speakers. Turn your headphones down, use closed-back ones, or move the mic closer and lower its gain.
Voice too quiet, even at fullGain too low at the source. Raise it in the mic app or interface first; use a Gain filter only as a last resort. Quiet dynamic mics on interfaces may need an inline preamp.
Voice sounds squashed, or breathing gets loudToo much compression. Raise the compressor's threshold a few dB or lower its ratio.
Words chopped at the start or endGate too high, or its release too short. Lower the gate numbers by 3 dB, or lengthen the release.
Keyboard clacks come throughMic too far away or pointing at the keyboard. Move it closer and aim its back at the keys; Noise Suppression (RNNoise or NVIDIA) helps.
Your lips and your voice don't line upCamera delay. In Advanced Audio Properties, add a Sync Offset to your mic (start around 100 to 200 ms) until they match in a recording.
Mic cuts out or vanishesUSB power saving or a changed device. Use a rear USB port, turn off USB selective suspend in Windows power options, and re-pick the device in the mic's properties.
Game suddenly very loud on streamWindows or the game changed volume, or Desktop Audio caught it twice. Use Application Audio Capture for the game and set its level in OBS.
