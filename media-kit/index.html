Where to find your numbers

  • Twitch: Creator Dashboard → Insights (Stream Summary and Channel Analytics) for average viewers, peak, hours streamed and watched.
  • Kick: your Creator Dashboard's stats pages.
  • YouTube and TikTok: YouTube Studio → Analytics, TikTok's Analytics in Creator tools; both show age and country.
  • Public stats sites such as Streams Charts and SullyGnome show your history too, and brands use them to check your numbers.
  • Use the last 30 or 90 days, say which, and update the kit every month or two.

Never round up or guess. Brands check, and one inflated number costs more than a small honest one. Small, engaged audiences get deals all the time.

Working with brands

  • Start with brands you already use. "I've used your headset on stream for a year" is a far stronger pitch than a cold ask, and your audience believes it.
  • Pitch an idea, not just yourself: a giveaway night, a segment, a setup tour. Make it easy to say yes.
  • Affiliate codes are a good first step. Many brands run open programmes, and results there make a paid deal easier to get.
  • Get it in writing: what you'll do, when, how they'll pay, and when. Read anything about exclusivity carefully.
  • Pricing: there's no fixed rate. Ask streamers your size what they charge, price per stream or per segment, and keep a minimum you won't go under.
  • Label it as an ad. In the UK the ASA and CMA expect paid promotion to be clearly marked (for example "Ad" or "#ad" up front), and the US FTC has similar rules. Twitch has a branded content option in Stream Manager; use whatever each platform gives you, and say it out loud on stream.
  • Watch for scams: "brand deals" that ask you to pay first, download a file, or log in through a strange link are scams. Real brands don't.
