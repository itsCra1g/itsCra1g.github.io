1

A workflow you'll actually keep up

  1. Clip while you're liveOn Twitch, Alt+X (or the clapperboard) grabs the last 90 seconds, so clip just after the moment. Put a clip button on your Stream Deck or a chat command for mods.
  2. Note the timeCan't clip? Say "clip that" or drop a stream marker, then find it in the VOD later.
  3. Pick your best threeAfter the stream, pick three moments. The one you'd tell a mate about is the one to post.
  4. Edit once, verticalOne 1080×1920 version with captions, using the layout below. Batch a week's worth in one sitting.
  5. Post everywhereThe same video to TikTok, Shorts and Reels, without another app's watermark.
  6. Learn from the numbersCheck which ones people watched to the end, and make more like those.

What makes a good clip: a clear payoff (a clutch, a fail, a big reaction, a funny line), understandable without context, and over in under a minute. If it needs "you had to be there", leave it.

4

Editing that works

  • Start on the payoff, or straight before it. Cut the build-up hard: most viewers decide in the first second or two.
  • 15 to 35 seconds is the sweet spot. Longer only if it stays interesting the whole way.
  • Burn captions in (most people watch with the sound off), big and in the middle third. Check the words: auto-captions get game terms, names and emotes wrong.
  • Your name on screen for the whole video, small, inside the safe area, so people know who to look for.
  • Zoom or punch in on the important moment, and keep your face visible for reactions.
  • Loop it: if the end flows back into the start, people watch it twice, which helps it spread.
  • Tools: CapCut (free, easy, auto-captions; Twitch's promo partner), DaVinci Resolve (free, more control), Twitch's own clip editor (trim, portrait crop and captions; no layers). AI clipping tools can find moments for you; check the results and the price.
5

Posting

  • Post the same video to all three, uploaded fresh each time. Don't repost a TikTok with its watermark on Shorts or Reels: platforms push those down.
  • Little and often: three or four a week you can keep up beats a burst of twenty then nothing.
  • Point people back: your link in bio, your schedule in a pinned comment, and "live [days] on Twitch and Kick" in captions.
  • Reply to comments in the first hour; it helps the video and builds the habit of people talking to you.
  • Music: copyrighted songs can get videos muted or blocked. Use each app's licensed sounds, royalty-free music, or none.
  • Clips other people make: on Twitch, anyone can clip your stream; you can delete clips from your channel in Creator Dashboard → Content → Clips.
