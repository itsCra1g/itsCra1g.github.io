The avatar and banner are simple starters to get you going. A logo or art made just for you will always stand out more, but even then, keep these colours and fonts so everything matches.

2

Your name

  • Same handle everywhere: Twitch, Kick, YouTube, TikTok, X, Discord, Instagram. Check they're all free before you commit, so people can find you anywhere with one search.
  • Easy to say and spell. If someone hears it on stream, can they type it? Swapping letters for numbers (like the 1 in itsCRA1G) works best when it's part of the joke or the look, not a workaround.
  • Leave room to grow. A name tied to one game is awkward when you change games.
  • Keep it private. Don't use your full real name, birth year or town.
3

Colour

  • One main colour does most of the work. Add a second for highlights, plus a dark or light background. Three colours is plenty.
  • Use the main colour sparingly (roughly 60% background, 30% neutral, 10% colour). A little colour looks branded; a lot looks loud.
  • Check contrast. Text in your colour has to be readable on your background. The builder's contrast check tells you whether your colour works for text or only for shapes and glows.
  • Write down the codes (like #4DA3FF) and use the exact same ones everywhere. "Roughly blue" in five places looks like five different blues.
4

Fonts

  • Two fonts at most: a heading font with personality, and a plain one for everything else.
  • Test it small. Your name has to read at the size of a chat message and on a phone.
  • Use fonts you're allowed to use. All the fonts here are from Google Fonts, which are free for any use, including on stream and in merch. Fonts from "free font" sites often aren't free for commercial use.
  • Pixel and comic fonts are fun for headings but hard to read in long text. Keep them for titles.
5

Logo and avatar

  • Your avatar shows tiny in chat, follows lists and notifications. A face, a simple mascot or one or two letters reads best; detailed art turns to mush.
  • Make it at 800×800 or bigger and it'll be sharp everywhere.
  • Want a custom logo? Ask an artist for your colours and font, a version that works on dark and light, and the original files.
  • Don't use other brands' logos, characters or artwork as your own. It can get your art taken down and causes headaches later.
6

Voice and identity

The strongest brands aren't just a look; they're a personality viewers can describe in three words.

  • Pick three words for how your stream feels (for example: chaotic, friendly, competitive). Check every title, panel and post against them.
  • Name your community. A name for your viewers, your Discord and your sub tiers gives people something to belong to.
  • Running jokes and catchphrases become emotes, alert lines, channel point rewards and merch. Write them down when they happen.
  • Use the same tone everywhere: your alerts, bot messages, Discord posts and socials should all sound like you.
